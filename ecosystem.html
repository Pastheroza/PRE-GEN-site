Ecosystem

One protocol,every seat at the table.

PRE-GEN is not a product — it is a division of labor. Five kinds of participants, five signed objects moving between them, and one shared outcome: AI-generated content that arrives with its permissions attached.

Who does what.

RIGHTS HOLDERS

The source of permission

Actors, public figures, brands, estates, voice owners, style holders. They register subjects, set terms and tiers, approve or refuse license requests — or file an unconditional opt-out. Their cryptographic key signs the license; nothing moves without it.

REGISTRY OPERATORS

The neutral counterparty

Run the registry: assign PG codes, sign every allow/deny decision, keep the hash-chained audit log, resolve disputes, verify rights-holder identity. PRAMPTA is the first commercial operator — the specification is operator-agnostic, and anyone may run one.

AI PROVIDERS

The ones who must ask

Model companies, voice platforms, image and video generators. They implement the three-call flow — detect, verify, prove — and keep the signed decisions. Compliance becomes a property of their pipeline, not of their promises.

CREATIVE PRODUCTIONS

The buyers of certainty

Film and TV studios, advertising agencies, game studios using generative tools. They need a clean chain of title for AI-generated scenes: E&O insurers, distributors, and broadcasters require documented rights — not vibes. PRE-GEN output is that documentation.

VERIFIERS

The trust check

Platforms, insurers, courts, journalists, anyone. They verify evidence bundles offline, resolve PG codes publicly at /lookup, and read embedded assertions inside C2PA manifests — years later, without trusting anyone’s word for it.

THE SPEC

The referee

Apache-2.0, byte-level test vectors, frozen identifier format. No single company owns the protocol — including us. Operators compete on service; the rules stay open.

How an authorized generation flows.

One scene in a film, generated with a licensed actor’s likeness. Six moves, five signed objects, zero ambiguity afterwards.

01
Register. The actor registers a subject on a registry. PG code PG-000042* assigned — permanent, check-summed.
02
License. The production requests a license; the actor approves. Scoped: film title, territory, media, time window. Signed by both sides. pg.license.v2
03
Ask. The studio’s generative pipeline detects the subject in a prompt and calls /v1/verify/. Signed decision comes back: allowed. pg.decision.v1
04
Generate & prove. The scene is produced; the provider files a receipt tying the output to the decision. pg.receipt.v2
05
Attach. A PRE-GEN assertion is embedded into the scene’s C2PA manifest — the permission travels inside the file. pg.assertion.v1
06
Clear. The studio exports the evidence bundle — decision, license, audit chain — into the chain-of-title file for insurers and distributors. pg.evidence.v1

Where PRE-GEN sits — and why RSL and C2PA are not it.

The honest map of the AI-trust stack has three layers, and they answer different questions. PRE-GEN does not compete with the other two; it completes them.

RSLC2PA / watermarkingPRE-GEN
Question answered“May AI crawl or train on my website?”“Who made this, and when?”“Was this generation authorized — before it happened?”
LayerInput — training & crawl accessOutput — provenance markingAuthorization — pre-generation consent
InstrumentLicense terms declared in robots.txt / rsl.txtSigned manifest inside the fileEd25519-signed decision + scoped license + hash-chained audit
Likeness, voice, brand?No — copyright-centric; right of publicity is out of scopeNo — labels origin, not permissionYes — subjects are people, voices, brands, styles, estates
Per-use proofNo — self-published terms, no per-use recordProvenance onlyYes — every generation leaves a signed, verifiable trail

“Why not just RSL?” Because RSL answers a question PRE-GEN doesn’t ask: whether a crawler may read your pages to train a model. It has no concept of a person, a likeness, a scoped film license, or a signed per-generation decision — and it cannot hand a studio a chain-of-title file. The reverse is also true: PRE-GEN does not govern crawlers. A complete stack uses both — RSL for the training layer, PRE-GEN for the generation layer. And where RSL declares terms for machines to read, PRE-GEN executes them: scoped tiers, auto-approval, license requests, and a signed answer per use.

Why the split between spec and operator matters.

PRE-GEN is an open specification; PRAMPTA is its first commercial registry. That separation is deliberate. If a studio’s legal department distrusts one operator, the protocol outlives that distrust — anyone can run a competing registry, and the signed objects verify the same way. Trust attaches to signatures and audit chains, not to a company’s word.
