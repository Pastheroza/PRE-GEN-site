FAQ

What is PRE-GEN?

An open protocol for asking permission before AI generates a real person, brand, voice or work. A registry answers with a signed decision; the provider keeps it and reports what it generated.

How is it different from C2PA?

C2PA describes what was generated and by whom. PRE-GEN asks, before the model runs, whether a registry’s records permit it. It checks declared rights and conditions; it does not decide what is lawful. A PRE-GEN assertion can travel inside a C2PA manifest.

Can it stop all unauthorized AI content?

No. It works only where providers integrate it. It cannot stop systems that never ask, local models or services that ignore it. Its value grows with the number of providers that ask.

What does not_blocked mean?

Personal use of a registered subject that nothing prohibits: no opt-out, no protection for minors, no fixed denial applies. It grants no licence and gives no right to publish commercially. It exists so personal use can be reported and counted.

What does an opt-out do?

It is checked before any licence. No licence can override it. Registering and opting out are free.

Who runs it?

Anyone may run a registry or integrate; the specification is Apache-2.0. Today one registry operates in production: PRAMPTA. See Registries.

What if a provider ignores a refusal?

The signed refusal and the audit chain show that the provider was told no, by whom, when and under which rules. PRE-GEN gives courts and platforms something they can verify; it does not enforce.

What does it cost to integrate?

The protocol, vectors and reference SDK are free. Registries set their own terms for credentials; PRAMPTA's sandbox is free.

How do signatures survive key rotation?

Every decision names the key fingerprint it was signed with. Registries keep old public keys in their key set so past decisions stay verifiable.

PRE-GEN is an open standard by Valerii Egorov, Apache-2.0. It is not owned by any registry, including PRAMPTA.
