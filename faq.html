FAQ

What is PRE-GEN?

An open way for an AI service to ask permission before it generates a real person, a character or a brand. The service asks the registry that represents them. The registry answers yes or no, signed, for that one request. The service generates only on a yes and keeps the answer as proof. A person's face and voice come first; characters and brands come next. The rules fit on one page.

How is it different from C2PA?

C2PA says what was generated and by whom, after the fact. PRE-GEN asks before the model runs. It checks what the registry's records permit; it does not decide what is lawful. A PRE-GEN proof can travel inside a C2PA manifest.

Can it stop all unauthorized AI content?

No. It works only where AI services choose to ask. It cannot stop a service that never asks, or a model running on someone's own computer. The more services ask, the more it is worth.

What does a signed answer prove?

That this registry gave this answer to this exact request, at that time, and that nobody changed it since. It does not prove that the output was lawful or that nothing was generated without asking. A ToG code written into a file proves nothing by itself. Only a signature that checks out against the registry directory does.

What does “not blocked” mean?

This registry holds no decision for this request: no opt-out, no protection for minors and no fixed refusal applies, and nothing permits it either. It is not a licence, gives no right to publish, and does not say the use is lawful. It exists so that personal use can be reported and counted.

What does an opt-out do?

A person's refusal is checked before any licence, and no licence can override it. Registering and opting out are free.

How do I create my ToG?

On Create your ToG: say what you need it for (your likeness, or a company's brand or products), answer a few questions, check your statement and choose a registry from the list of available registries. The registry issues your ToG code. You then publish your ToG statement on a website or profile you control, or keep only the code for a product. Under 18, only a parent or legal guardian can make a statement, and only a No. An AI service needs no code: it connects instead.

Is pregen.org a registry?

No. pregen.org keeps the protocol, the Licence text, the signed list of registries and the form at /tog/create. It registers no person or company and holds no codes. Registries do: you choose one, it confirms your identity and your rights, and after that your ToG code takes effect for every registry and AI service on PRE-GEN Protocol.

Who runs it?

Anyone can run a registry or add PRE-GEN to an AI service. The specification is Apache-2.0, and the open reference registry starts a complete registry in two commands. Today one registry runs in production: PRAMPTA. See Registries.

Who owns PRE-GEN?

No company. The author, Valerii Egorov, also runs PRAMPTA, the first registry, which has no rights that any other registry lacks. His written promise (the paper, §15): as soon as an independent, international non-profit exists that can take PRE-GEN, it gets the standard, the key and the last word. The successor key for that hand-over is already published. If he doesn't, anyone may officially call him a bitch, in public, with a link to that paragraph. His words.

Can someone set up a fake registry and sell licences for a celebrity?

A registry that is not in the signed directory is refused everywhere. A copy of a real registry with its own key is refused too. A listed registry that claims a person it cannot prove loses to a claim the person confirmed on their own official channel. If nobody confirmed anything, the result is “not blocked”: it permits nothing and refuses nothing, so an impostor can neither permit nor freeze a person. Complaints are answered within seven days, the steward can mark a false claim in the directory, and a registry that keeps doing it is removed: nothing it ever signed permits anything again. And a licence from someone without the rights gives no rights at law.

Someone put a ToG code and the word “licensed” on a fake video. Does it pass?

No. A code is just text. What counts is the signed proof that travels with the file (proof): the registry's yes, the service's report naming this exact file, and the registry's acknowledgement. A proof copied onto another video points to a different file. An edited report no longer matches what the registry signed.

The same person is in two registries. Who wins?

Not whoever came first. Whoever the person confirmed. A claim confirmed on the person's own official channel beats unconfirmed ones. Among confirmed claims, a refusal of the person in any registry holds in all of them. With no confirmed claim the result is “not blocked”, which permits nothing. A matching name is never evidence (identity).

Can a registry permit intimate content without the person's own consent?

No. Sexual or adult content needs the person's own signature on the licence, a verified identity, and a licence that names those categories. The registry cannot sign for them.

Do I have to go through PRAMPTA or any other middleman?

No. An AI service and a registry talk directly, free. Services that make it easier are optional, and anyone may offer them.

What if a service ignores a refusal?

The signed refusal shows that the service was told no, by whom, when and under which rules. PRE-GEN gives courts and platforms something they can verify; it does not enforce.

What does it cost?

The protocol, schemas, test vectors, verification libraries and the reference registry are free. Asking a registry and getting its signed answer is free too, with no account. A registry may sell licences at its own prices; that is its own business, separate from PRE-GEN.

What happens when a registry changes its keys?

Every answer names the key that signed it. Registries keep old public keys, and the directory keeps every key a registry ever used, so past answers stay checkable.
