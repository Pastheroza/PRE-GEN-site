What it proves

For AI services and their lawyers · PRE-GEN v5 · the full statement of limits is PRE-GEN-SPEC.md §10 (informative); the rules are PRE-GEN-CORE.md

PRE-GEN is permission before generation, and evidence of it, among the services that choose to ask. It does not stop services that never ask, and it does not decide whether a use is lawful.

SayDo not say
"We query PRE-GEN before generation and keep the signed decisions and receipts.""PRE-GEN guarantees the use is lawful." "Nobody can generate this likeness anymore."

Refusals and opt-outs

  • An opt-out has priority over every licence in the registry that holds it. Under the identity extension, a client that finds the same person, confirmed on their own channel, in several registries applies a refusal from any of them.
  • A new opt-out waits 14 days at PRAMPTA before it takes effect, because anyone claiming authority can file one. The subject's owner can pause the subject immediately.
  • From its effective time every new decision refuses. A decision issued earlier stays usable until it expires (300 seconds at PRAMPTA); a cached allow never outlives its expiry.
  • Every answer about a subject, refusals included, is a signed answer you can keep. A request the registry cannot authenticate gets HTTP 401 (PG_NOT_AUTHENTICATED on /v2/requests, invalid_credential on /v1/verify), which is not signed.
  • PG_UNKNOWN_SUBJECT (PG_NO_SUBJECT on /v1) is not a prohibition. It says only that this registry does not know the subject; the user may hold rights it does not record. Decide under your own policy, and do not present the output as licensed by that registry.

What to say plainly

  • Systems that never ask — offline, open-weight or non-integrated models — are outside the protocol.
  • Recognition is the provider's job. Authorization is asked for a named subject; resemblance, style or composites without a name are not caught by PRE-GEN.
  • Managed custody (the reference registry today): consent rests on the registry's records, not on a key the subject holds.
  • A false allow from a compromised registry cannot be independently confirmed or ruled out without a verifiable licence and a trusted subject key.
  • One registry in production today (PRAMPTA). Codes from several registries never collide. When a provider knows two registries hold the same person and they disagree, neither answer decides by itself; the provider keeps both and does not call the output licensed while a refusal is unresolved. For a public person with anchors, the identity rule decides instead: only claims the person confirmed count; a person has one home registry, and two confirmed homes are a duplicate in which the stricter answer decides. Registries that declare the identity extension publish anchors (GET /v2/anchors) that tell a provider when two registries hold the same person; PRAMPTA does not publish them yet.
  • A listing is not a rights check. The steward admits and can revoke registries; whether each rights holder is genuine is the registry's responsibility. The decision's subject_authority says how well the registry established it: only verified means the registry checked identity and authority.
  • C2PA: the signed assertion exists, but an end-to-end C2PA integration has not been tested.
  • Licences are sold at PRAMPTA at prices the rights holder sets. Decisions and receipts work the same whether or not a licence was bought.
