Getting listed

The directory is what keeps fake registries out: verification accepts a registry's signatures only for the prefix the signed directory gives it. A listing says the registry met the conditions below. It does not certify that everyone the registry represents is genuine. That stays the registry's responsibility.

Registries open a registry application and receive a prefix for their PG codes. Bring:

  1. a named operator, a company or a person, with a public address and a contact for complaints;
  2. a published policy: how you check that someone is who they say and may license the subject, your own refusal rules, answer lifetime, opt-out waiting period, and a public identifier (Wikidata, ISNI or a domain) for every public person you register;
  3. acceptance of the core rules: the protections no licence can override, the rule for a person held by several registries, and an answer to every complaint within seven days;
  4. a public API following the core (POST /v2/requests), a key set at /keys and a document at /.well-known/pregen-registry.json; your key fingerprints given to the steward directly, and your next key announced;
  5. open access for AI services: answer any AI app known by its domain, with no account, on the same terms for all (provider-identity). You may also issue your own credentials, and you may check an app before you answer it fully. Registries listed earlier have 90 days from this rule's publication;
  6. a passing run of the published test vectors and schemas.

AI services are listed by the registry they work with, after a real request and matching report.

Revocation

The steward can remove a registry that licenses people or works it has no right to, ignores proven complaints, breaks the core rules, or is compromised. A removed registry stays in the directory forever, its prefix is never reused, and nothing signed by its keys permits a generation again. A single stolen key can be removed the same way while the registry keeps its other keys. A removal is never undone, and each one is announced here with its reason. Verifiers see it the next time they load a current directory, at least daily.

Working together

Every code belongs to exactly one registry. Each registry issues codes under its own prefix, so codes never collide. Bare PG-… codes issued before version 5 keep their meaning; no registry issues new ones. A registry asked about another registry's code points here.

  • Who proved it, not who came first. When several registries claim the same public person, a claim the person confirmed on their own official channel wins. A refusal of the person in any registry holds in all of them. Several unconfirmed claims stop generation until someone proves one. A matching name is never evidence.
  • Complaints. A registry answers within seven days: a first response and protective steps such as a freeze, not necessarily a final decision. The steward can record a proven false claim in the directory. Repeated false claims are grounds for removal.
  • No middleman. AI services and registries talk directly. Services that make it easier are optional and open to anyone.
