Getting listed

A listing is the one place where PRE-GEN keeps a fake registry out: verification libraries accept a registry's signatures only when the steward-signed directory lists it for that namespace. A listing is the steward's statement that a registry met these conditions, not a certificate that its rights holders are genuine — that stays the registry's responsibility.

Registries open an issue in the specification repository and receive a four-letter prefix (PG codes). Bring:

  1. a named operator — a company or a person — with a public address and a contact for complaints and disputes;
  2. a published policy: how you verify that a rights holder is who they claim and may license the subject, your own floors and refusal codes, answer lifetime, opt-out waiting period — and an anchor (Wikidata, ISNI or a domain) for every public person you register;
  3. acceptance of the core: its floor of protections, the rule for a person held by several registries, and an answer to every complaint within seven days;
  4. a public API following the core (POST /v2/requests), a key set at /keys and a document at /.well-known/pregen-registry.json, your key fingerprints given out of band, your next key announced;
  5. a passing run of the published test vectors and schemas.

Providers are listed by the registry they integrate with, after a production decision and matching receipt.

Revocation

The steward can revoke a registry that licenses people or works it has no authority over, ignores proven complaints, breaks the core floor, or is compromised. A revoked registry stays in the directory forever, its prefix is never reused, and nothing signed by its keys authorizes a generation again — verifiers see it when they next load a current directory, at least daily. A single stolen key can be revoked the same way while the registry keeps its other keys. A revocation can never be undone, and each one is announced here with its reason. Verification libraries from version 0.5.0 enforce it; a verifier learns of it the next time it fetches the directory.

Working together

Every code belongs to exactly one registry. Each registry issues codes under its own four-letter prefix, so codes never collide. Bare PG-… codes issued before version 5 keep their meaning; no registry issues new ones. A registry asked about another registry's code points to this list.

  • Who proved it, not who came first. When several registries claim the same public person, a claim the person confirmed on their own official channel silences unconfirmed claims. A refusal of the person in any of their registries holds in all of them. Several unconfirmed claims freeze: nobody permits until someone proves it. A matching name is never evidence.
  • Disputes. A registry answers a complaint within seven days — a first response and protective measures such as a freeze, not necessarily a final decision. The steward can record an established false claim in the directory; clients drop it when they load that directory. Repeated false claims are ground for revocation.
  • No intermediary required. Providers and registries exchange requests and answers directly. Services that make it easier are optional and open to anyone.

Not yet specified: settlement between registries, and profiles for works and characters.
