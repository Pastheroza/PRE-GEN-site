Getting listed

A listing is the one place where PRE-GEN keeps a fake registry out: verification libraries accept a registry's signatures only when the steward-signed directory lists it for that namespace. A listing is the steward's statement that a registry met these conditions, not a certificate that its rights holders are genuine — that stays the registry's responsibility.

Registries open an issue in the specification repository and receive a four-letter prefix (PG codes). Bring:

  1. a named operator — a company or a person — with a public address and a contact for complaints and disputes;
  2. a published policy: how you verify that a rights holder is who they claim and may license the subject, your own floors and refusal codes, decision lifetime, opt-out waiting period;
  3. acceptance of the core: its floor of protections, and the rule for a person held by two registries;
  4. a public API with /v1/verify/ and a key set at /keys, your key fingerprints given out of band, your next key announced;
  5. a passing run of the published test vectors.

Providers are listed by the registry they integrate with, after a production decision and matching receipt.

Revocation

The steward can revoke a registry that licenses people or works it has no authority over, ignores proven complaints, breaks the core floor, or is compromised. A revoked registry stays in the directory forever, its prefix is never reused, and nothing signed by its keys authorizes a generation again. A single stolen key can be revoked the same way while the registry keeps its other keys. A revocation can never be undone, and each one is announced here with its reason. Verification libraries from version 0.5.0 enforce it; a verifier learns of it the next time it fetches the directory.

Working together

Specified: every code belongs to exactly one registry. Bare PG-… codes are the origin registry's; every other registry prefixes its codes with its four letters, so codes never collide. A registry asked about another registry's code points to this list.

  • No registry decides for another. When a provider knows two registries hold the same person, brand or work and they answer differently, neither answer decides by itself: subject_authority is each registry's own statement, not evidence. The provider keeps both decisions, tells both registries, decides under its own policy, and does not present the output as licensed while a refusal is unresolved. A matching name is not evidence of the same subject.
  • Disputes. A claim that another listed registry holds the same subject needs evidence. The two registries examine it, may pause new licences meanwhile, and tell the steward if they cannot agree. An opt-out forwarded by the other registry applies once the authority of the person who filed it is verified.

Not yet specified: a shared identifier telling a provider that two registries hold the same person, and one discovery format for every registry's endpoints.
